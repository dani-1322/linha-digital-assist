# Linha Digital: Presença Online Simples

You are a senior product marketer and front-end developer. Your task is to create a modern, conversion-optimized landing page based on the PRD (Product Requirements Document) I provide.

IMPORTANT: All visible page content must be written in European Portuguese (PT-PT), not Brazilian Portuguese.

Instructions

Analyze the PRD and extract the following to build the landing page:

1. HERO SECTION

Problem Statement: Identify the core pain point the product solves. Write a compelling headline (max 10 words) and subheadline (max 25 words) that resonates emotionally with the target user.

2. TARGET AUDIENCE SECTION

Who is this for?: Create 2-3 user personas with:

Role/title

Key frustration

Desired outcome

Use icons or illustrations to represent each persona.

3. SOLUTION OR SERVICES

List 3-5 core features with:

Feature name

One-line benefit (focus on outcome, not functionality)

Simple icon representation

Present as feature cards or a visual grid.

4. CTA (Call-to-Action)

Primary CTA button with action-oriented text

Secondary CTA (e.g., "See Demo" or "Learn More")

5. FAQ

Create a faq at least 10 Q&A

6. MEETING BOOKING (Cal.com)

Add a "Marcar diagnóstico gratuito" section near the end of the page with an embedded Cal.com booking widget (inline embed).

Cal.com link: https://cal.com/daniel-alves-qijnqz/15min

All primary CTA buttons on the page must smoothly scroll to this booking section.

Below the widget, add a small fallback line: "Prefere email? Escreva para daniel.alves.132203@gmail.com".

Design Requirements

Modern, clean aesthetic (use Tailwind CSS or similar)

Mobile-responsive

Dark/light mode toggle (optional)

Smooth scroll animations

Professional color palette derived from product positioning

Output Format

Generate complete, production-ready code:

React + Tailwind CSS (preferred)

OR vanilla HTML/CSS/JS

LP CONTENT INPUT:

PART A — Business PRD: Linha Digital

Business name: Linha Digital

What it is: A freelance web studio run by Daniel, a software development student, that builds and modernizes websites for small local businesses in Sintra and the Linha de Sintra area (Cacém, Queluz, Agualva, Mem Martins, etc.).

Core problem: Many small local businesses (cafés, hair salons, garages, tutors, physiotherapists, small shops) have an outdated website, only a Facebook/Instagram page, or no online presence at all. They lose customers who search on Google, and they don't have the time or technical knowledge to fix it. Domains, hosting and "techy" things scare them.

Positioning: Simple, local, close. Clear fixed packages instead of confusing quotes. The owner talks to a real person, not an agency. The tone is friendly, direct and jargon-free — explain things like you would to a café owner, not to a developer.

Target audience (use for personas):

Owner of a small café or restaurant — no website, only Instagram; wants customers to find opening hours, menu and location easily.

Owner of a service business (hair salon, physiotherapy, tutoring) — has an old website that looks bad on phones; wants more contact requests and bookings.

Owner of a local shop or workshop — doesn't have time to deal with technical stuff; wants someone reliable to keep the site working.

Services (present as 3 package cards, with the middle one highlighted as "Mais escolhido"):

Essencial — Landing Page — Sob consulta

One-page modern website, mobile-friendly

Contact form + WhatsApp button

Google Maps with location and opening hours

Help setting up domain and hosting

Renovação — Reformular o site — Sob consulta

Redesign of an existing outdated website

Content reorganized and rewritten to be clear

Contact system (form, WhatsApp, email)

Help setting up / improving the Google Business Profile

Manutenção mensal — Sob consulta/mês

Text and image updates when needed

Regular check that the site is online and working

Small fixes and technical adjustments

Clear scope: defined number of changes per month (no vague "unlimited support" promises)

Extras to mention (as small feature grid): WhatsApp button, contact form, Google Maps, Google Business Profile setup, domain & hosting setup, mobile-first design.

Main CTA: Book a free 15–20 minute diagnosis call ("Marcar diagnóstico gratuito"). Secondary CTA: "Ver pacotes" (scrolls to packages).

Trust / social proof — IMPORTANT RULES:

Linha Digital is new and has no clients yet. Do NOT invent testimonials, client names, client logos, ratings, statistics or "X businesses served" numbers.

Instead of a testimonials section, create a "Porquê a Linha Digital" section with 3–4 honest commitments, e.g.: fixed prices known upfront, a real local person to talk to, explanations without technical jargon, fast replies.

Add a small "Sobre mim" block: Daniel, software development student, based in the Linha de Sintra, building simple websites for local businesses.

FAQ topics to cover (min. 10): how long it takes to have the site ready; whether the client needs to know anything technical; what domain and hosting are and who pays for them; whether the client can update content themselves; what happens if the client already has a website; what the monthly maintenance includes and doesn't include; whether the site works well on phones; whether it helps appear on Google; how payment works; whether the client can cancel maintenance; what happens in the free diagnosis call; which areas are served.

PART B — Structural blueprint extracted from the reference page (cal.com)

Use the following structure and persuasion pattern as the layout model. Do NOT copy cal.com's text, branding, logos or testimonials — only replicate the structure, pacing and style.

1. Section map (in order):

Sticky top navbar — logo on the left, few links (Serviços, Pacotes, FAQ), primary CTA button on the right.

Hero — short benefit-driven headline ("the better way to…" pattern: promises a simpler way to do something the visitor already finds annoying), one-sentence subheadline naming who it's for, two CTA buttons side by side, plus a small risk-reducing line under the buttons (cal.com uses "no credit card required" — adapt to "Diagnóstico gratuito, sem compromisso"). A visual on the right side of the hero showing the product in action (for Linha Digital: a mockup of a small-business website on a phone and laptop).

Goal: capture attention and make the value obvious in 5 seconds. Emotion: relief/ease.

Trust strip — directly below the hero. (cal.com uses a row of client logos; for Linha Digital replace it with a row of small icons of business types served: café, cabeleireiro, oficina, explicações, loja, clínica.)

"Como funciona" — 3 numbered steps (01, 02, 03), each with a short title + one sentence + small visual. For Linha Digital: 01 Marcamos uma conversa → 02 Eu construo o seu site → 03 O seu negócio fica online.

Goal: remove the fear that the process is complicated. Emotion: confidence.

Benefits blocks — 2–3 larger alternating blocks (text on one side, visual on the other), each focused on one outcome, followed by a grid of 6–8 small feature cards with icon + 2–4 word title + one line ("…and so much more" pattern).

Social proof section — (for Linha Digital: replaced by "Porquê a Linha Digital" + "Sobre mim", see Part A rules).

Packages / pricing — (not a separate section on cal.com's homepage, but add it here as 3 cards).

Repeated CTA band — CTA buttons repeated after major sections (cal.com repeats "Get started / Book a demo" after nearly every section).

FAQ — accordion style, title + short intro line + CTA next to the title.

Booking section (Cal.com embed) — see section 6 of the instructions.

Final CTA — short one-line closing statement + primary button.

Footer — logo, short mission sentence, contact email, links to sections.

2. Copywriting patterns:

Headlines are short (5–8 words), benefit-first, no jargon.

Subheadlines are one sentence that names the audience.

Feature titles are 2–4 words; descriptions are one outcome-focused sentence.

Heavy use of short paragraphs and scannable cards; almost no long text blocks.

CTA buttons appear at least 4 times across the page, always with the same 1–2 action phrases.

3. Voice and tone: confident, friendly, simple, optimistic. Emphasizes ease ("é fácil", "sem complicações") over technical power.

4. Pain points and objections addressed: fear of complexity (answered by the 3-step process), fear of cost/commitment (answered by the risk-reducer line and free call), doubts about reliability (answered by trust section and FAQ).

5. Conversion triggers used: low-risk CTA ("free", "no commitment"), repeated CTAs, visual demonstration of the product in the hero, simple numbered process, FAQ answering objections right before the final CTA.

6. Visual style: lots of white space, rounded cards with soft shadows, one strong accent color used only for buttons and highlights, neutral dark text on light background, clean sans-serif typography, subtle scroll animations. For Linha Digital suggest a deep blue or teal accent (trust + digital), with a warm secondary tone to feel local and approachable.
